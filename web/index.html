The Big Picture

15 years of Oxford PPE results at a glance.

How it works

Each paper's mark distribution is modelled using a latent ability factor model. The idea: students have some underlying ability level, and each paper's marks reflect that ability plus paper-specific noise.

  • Paper fits: 81 papers fitted to truncated normal distributions using 15 years of examiner band data (excluding 2020).
  • Ability slider: shifts all your expected marks up or down, like moving along the ability distribution of all PPE students.
  • Simulation: 50,000 exam sittings are drawn, each classified using the official PPE conjunctive rules (average thresholds + paper count requirements).
  • Uncertainty: estimates are roughly ±3 percentage points — treat them as priors, not predictions.

Methodology

The model

Each paper's mark distribution is modelled as:

mark_i = μ_i + λ_i·θ + ε_i

  • μ_i — paper-specific mean, fitted via MLE on truncated normals from 15 years of examiners' report band data.
  • θ — standardised latent ability. The ability slider sets θ = Φ⁻¹(percentile).
  • λ_i = σ_i·√ρ — ability loading proportional to paper spread. Higher-variance papers are more sensitive to ability (ρ ≈ 0.20, calibrated to match observed classification rates).
  • ε_i ~ N(0, σ²_i·(1−ρ)) — residual exam-day noise.

Classification rules

PPE uses conjunctive classification — you need both an average threshold and a minimum number of papers at that level:

ClassAverage ≥Additional requirement
1st68.5≥ 2 marks of 70+, no mark below 50
2.159.0≥ 3 marks of 60+
2.249.0≥ 3 marks of 50+
3rd40.0≥ 3 marks of 40+
Pass30.0—

Simulation

The simulation draws 50,000 samples, classifies each using the rules above, and reports the empirical distribution. This Monte Carlo approach captures the non-linear interactions between the conjunctive rules and paper-specific variance — something a simple normal approximation would miss.

Data

Data comes from Oxford PPE Final Honour School internal examiners' reports, 2011–2025. Mark distributions are extracted from band data (percentage of candidates in each class band per paper). 81 papers are fitted; 65 via MLE on band data, 16 via method-of-moments from reported means and standard deviations.

The year 2020 (COVID) is excluded from all fitting — the first rate roughly doubled that year despite paper-level marks barely changing, suggesting the anomaly was at classification stage.

2023 (marking boycott) has no per-paper data available.

Limitations

  • Uses aggregate band data, not individual marks — the true joint distribution is unobservable.
  • Assumes a single ability factor with constant ρ across all paper pairs. In reality, Philosophy papers likely correlate more with each other than with Economics papers.
  • No conditioning on prior performance — the ability slider is a self-assessment, not a calibrated prediction.
  • Temporal trends are ignored in the simulation (papers are fitted on pooled data).
  • Estimates are priors — they tell you what has happened historically for similar paper combinations, not what will happen to you.
